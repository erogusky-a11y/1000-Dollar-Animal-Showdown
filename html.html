    How the fight works

    Every number on the cards comes from the stats sheet: weight ranges, top speed, bite force or primary weapon, intelligence grade, and group dynamic. The game turns those into fight stats with a few fixed rules.

    • Health grows with the midpoint of the weight range, with diminishing returns so the biggest animals aren’t untouchable.
    • Attack also grows with weight, then gets boosted by bite force (more psi, more damage) and by intelligence grade (S is highest, D lowest). Elephants, Cape buffalo, and bison have no bite figure listed, so they get a flat charge-and-horn bonus instead.
    • Speed decides who arrives first and who strikes first each round.
    • Teamwork: if you hire two or more animals, each one’s attack rises with its group dynamic (Extreme most, None not at all).
    • Loners: animals listed as solitary (group dynamic None) or Minimal sometimes lash out at your own teammates.
    • The rest: enemies arrive two per round, fastest first. Each one sizes up your strongest guard and may back off. Because they’re different species with no reason to cooperate, they sometimes fight each other instead of you.
    • Guarding: each of your animals can block two attackers per round. If more enemies come at you than your team can block, one gets through and you lose.
    • Terrain: the crocodile is strong on a riverbank and weak on dry land (the sheet lists it at 18 mph swimming but 8 mph running). The jaguar gains at the river and on high ground, and the cougar gains on high ground, matching their combat profiles.

    Each fight has randomness built in, so the 1,000-fight simulation gives a better sense of a team’s real odds than a single run.
